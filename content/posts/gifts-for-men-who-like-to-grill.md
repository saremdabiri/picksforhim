## What do you buy a man who likes to grill?

Find out what he cooks on, then give him the thing he doesn't have. A man with a gas grill often wants to try smoking (a pellet grill) or flat-top cooking (a griddle). A man with a smoker usually wants a better thermometer. If you don't know his setup, a thermometer or a quality tool set works with every grill, and a spice set is an easy add-on that never goes unused.

## Charcoal, pellet, gas or griddle: which grill gift should you pick?

Charcoal (the Weber Kettle) gives the most traditional, smoky live-fire cooking. Weber's guide to the [two-zone fire](https://amer.weber.com/en-us/blogs/grill-skills/fire-configurations) explains how one kettle can sear steaks over direct heat and cook ribs or whole chickens on the indirect side. Pellet grills (Traeger, Ninja Woodfire) hold a set temperature for hours, which makes smoking easy. A propane griddle (Blackstone) is the fastest way to cook breakfast or burgers for a crowd.

## Why is a meat thermometer the best gift for a guy who grills?

Doneness is the hardest part of grilling, and a probe solves it. FoodSafety.gov lists safe minimum internal temperatures of 145°F for steaks and roasts with a 3-minute rest, 160°F for ground beef and 165°F for poultry ([FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures)). An instant-read like the Alpha Grillers checks burgers in seconds; a wireless probe like the MEATER Plus watches a brisket for hours.
