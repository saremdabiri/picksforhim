## What do you buy a man who likes to cook?

Buy the better version of a tool he already uses. Most men who cook already own a pan, a cheap knife and a grill, so a gift lands when it improves one of those: a sharper knife, a pot that lasts decades, or a thermometer that takes the guessing out of steak. Novelty gadgets end up in a drawer; a great knife or smoker gets used every weekend.

## Why is a meat thermometer the best gift for a guy who grills?

Because doneness is the hardest part of grilling, and a probe solves it. FoodSafety.gov lists safe minimum internal temperatures of 145°F for beef steaks and roasts with a 3-minute rest, 160°F for ground beef and 165°F for poultry ([FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures)). A wireless probe like the MEATER Plus shows the number on his phone, so he can take the steak off at the right moment.

## Grill, smoker or pizza oven: which outdoor gift should you pick?

Match it to his space. A Traeger Pro 22 is the classic choice for a big backyard and long smoking sessions. The Ninja Woodfire ProConnect fits a patio or deck and still smokes with real wood pellets. If he already has a grill, the Ninja Artisan pizza oven adds something new instead of replacing what he has. ## Tips for giving a cooking gift

- **Check his setup first.** Look at what's already in the kitchen or on the patio so you don't duplicate it.
- **Pair small with big.** A knife plus a cutting board, or a smoker plus a meat thermometer, makes a gift set.
- **Mind the space.** Measure the patio or counter before buying a grill or countertop oven.
- **Include the care note.** Japanese knives and wood boards need hand washing; tell him in the card.
